A Harrisburg resident asked a community group to recommend someone local who could build an affordable website. The request ended with “No apps please.” One reply suggested downloading Wix.

If you’re hiring a web designer in Harrisburg, PA, the phrase “four-page website” deserves a follow-up. Will the designer interview you about your work and turn that conversation into four finished pages? Or will you spend Sunday night at the kitchen table writing them? Have the proposal spell out who writes, who supplies photos and how many rounds of edits are included.

The $1,700 example comes to $2,060 for the build and the first 12 months before a domain or extras. Ask each designer for that first-year total and what you’ll pay in year two. Then look closely at any feature you’re counting on. A salon asking for “online booking” could get a form that requests a callback. If you want clients to see open appointments, choose a time and pay a deposit, put those steps in the quote.

Let a customer question decide what to build

Imagine you’re an electrician in Enola and a homeowner asks, “Do you install EV chargers in Camp Hill?” You already have the makings of a useful service page. Give it a title like “Home EV Charger Installation,” show a photo from a job you’ve finished, name the towns you serve and give the homeowner a way to request an estimate. If a proposal includes local search engine optimization (SEO), ask which customer questions the designer plans to answer this way.

If customers already find that page but call to say the estimate form won’t work on their phones, get a price for fixing the form before committing to a redesign.

Try the designer’s work on your own phone

Ask for a live site with a feature you need and give yourself a customer’s errand. For a restaurant, try to read the dinner menu and find Saturday’s hours. For a shop, see whether you can tell which items are available for local pickup. Use your phone on cellular data. Notice where you have to zoom in, wait or guess what to tap.

When your own preview is ready, test both sides of an inquiry. Fill out the form, read the confirmation and check the inbox your staff uses. At a computer, try the form using only the keyboard. If analytics are included, have the designer show you where the completed inquiry appears. After launch, you can compare those inquiries with the jobs you actually book.

Agree on the launch and the handoff

Fathom estimates about a week for its focused package. WebTek puts most of its builds at eight to ten weeks, depending on scope. Ask when you can try a working version and when customers can use it. Picture the Friday before a holiday. You need to change Saturday’s hours. Can you sign in and make that edit? If you’d prefer to call someone, who answers and what will it cost? Ask for the domain to be registered in an account your business controls. Get a written list of the code, content and logins you’ll receive, plus any licenses you’d need to keep paying for if you moved the site.
